The Civic Code of Individual Liberty

A Rule-Based Framework for Civic Engagement and Decentralized Governance

Core Mandate

Individual liberty first.

To protect individual liberty, enforce strict fiscal discipline, reject the corrupting influence of the two-party duopoly, and systematically dismantle the machinery of state expansion in favor of voluntary, non-coercive community cooperation.

Part I

Candidate Selection & Representative Accountability

You reject party-line branding at all levels of government, substituting marketing with personal knowledge or strict ideological alignment to ensure state power is never expanded.

Rule 1

The Local Exception

You will evaluate and vote for individual candidates only at the local municipal and county levels, where governance is closest to the citizen and track records are transparent.

Rule 2

The Higher-Office Boycott

You will completely withhold your vote for any candidate running for State or Federal office, effectively boycotting the major-party infrastructure and political marketing machine.

Rule 3

The Exception of Personal Knowledge & Trust

Condition: You may override Rules 1 and 2 to vote for a candidate at any level, including State or Federal, only if you personally know the individual, respect their true intentions, and trust their character to resist state expansion.

Rule 4

The Allowance of Compromise

Under the specific exception of Rule 3, you are willing to compromise on secondary policy points, recognizing that personal integrity and a shared fundamental worldview are a strong enough foundation to warrant cooperation.

Rule 5

The Third-Party Outlet

You may vote for a third-party candidate, principally Libertarian, if you truly agree with their platform. If a third-party candidate demands compromises on issues you deem non-negotiable, you will choose the “no vote” option.

Part II

Public Finance, Taxation, and Levies

Your ballot is a defensive wall against the financial expansion of the state.

Rule 6

The Fiscal Default

You will automatically vote NO on all new levies, new taxes, and extensions of existing temporary taxes. The burden of proof rests entirely on the state to operate within its current means. A 51% majority should never possess the moral authority to force a 49% minority to fund an initiative against their will.

Rule 7

The Marital Exception to Voluntary Harmony

You reserve the right to relent and allow a “Yes” vote or a yard sign for specific local initiatives, such as the Delaware County Board of Developmental Disabilities, exclusively out of respect for your wife's beliefs and personal connection to the cause. This represents a conscious household compromise, not an abandonment of your core code.

Rule 8

The Repeal Clause

You will vote YES on the removal or expiration of existing levies and taxes, provided you fully understand the mechanism of the repeal and its structural impact.

Part III

Bureaucracy & Regulations

You treat regulations as direct restrictions on personal and economic liberty.

Rule 9

Regulatory Containment

You will automatically vote NO on any ballot measure or initiative that introduces new codes, compliance mandates, or regulatory authority.

Rule 10

Regulatory Deconstruction

You will vote YES on measures that explicitly repeal, downscale, or eliminate existing regulations, expanding the sphere of individual choice.

Part IV

Property Expression & Visual Independence

Your physical property is an extension of your personal sovereignty and must remain free from state coercion branding.

Rule 11

The Campaign Sign Prohibition

You ban all political campaign and candidate signage from your personal property. Your lawn will not serve as a free billboard for political brands trying to win power over others.

Rule 12

The Generic Support Exception

You explicitly permit generic, non-political signs that celebrate community services, for example, “I Support the Delaware County Board of Developmental Disabilities.” This distinguishes voluntary, humanistic support for a cause from political endorsement of its tax-enforced funding.

Part V

The Voluntary Crowdfunded Levy Framework

Proposed System Reform

You advocate for a complete structural overhaul of the ballot financing system to replace state coercion with unanimous-consent funding models.

Rule 13

The Exemption Right

Any ballot measure or levy requiring public funding must operate on an opt-in framework. A “No” vote constitutes an immediate, permanent tax exemption from that specific initiative.

Rule 14

The Proportional Funding Standard

“Yes” voters shall only be liable for the baseline fraction they would have owed had the measure received 100% community support. The state cannot inflate an individual's tax liability to cover non-participants.

Rule 15

The Crowdfunding Threshold Mandate

Every funding initiative must include a legally binding minimum participation threshold, Variable “X.” If the final tally of “Yes” votes fails to meet this designated threshold, the initiative is automatically voided, and no citizens, including the “Yes” voters, are charged.
